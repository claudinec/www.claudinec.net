It's the time of year when bloggers post their end-of-year reflections. My blogging reflection for the year is that I inadvertently neglected my blog and much of the indieweb for a good ten months.

As I did during the previous global crisis of 2020–2021, I tried to resist feelings of helplessness by seeking, preserving, and sharing knowledge. At the start of 2025 I joined the [Safeguarding Research](https://forum.safeguar.de/about) initiative to preserve research data and cultural heritage endangered by the incoming US government. But closely following these developments led me to quietly burn out from the project (which is still active, thanks to collective action).

Most of my online activity this year has been on the English-language Wikipedia, which may be one of the web's few remaining defences against disinformation. I was part of the first big wave of editors who joined in July 2001, but after dabbling for a few years my attention turned to trying to figure out my career and personal identity in the "real world". I crawled back during the COVID-19 pandemic lockdowns, then disappeared again when I started adjusting to a [new job]({{< ref "/posts/2023-06-22-status" >}}). I became more active again in July 2024 and the habit seems to have stuck. I've been granted more permissions and responsibilities in quality control, started assisting new Wikipedians with publishing new content, and spend most days chatting in the various Discord servers for Wikimedians.[^discord]

[^discord]: Yes I know, Discord is a closed, extractive, corporate platform, but the Wikimedian IRC channels just don't see much activity these days.

The principles of the 1990s/2000s internet live on through the Wikimedia and IndieWeb movements, and it's no coincidence that these are both often described as *movements* or *communities*, rather than *platforms* or *services*. Both are living examples of grassroots, people-first projects to keep the web authentically human. The Wikimedia projects need a non-profit foundation to support its technical infrastructure and legal defences, but large sites like the English-language Wikipedia continue to be led by volunteers. Coming back to Wikipedia after a long hiatus, I saw that it had grown up to become *the* principal example of a modern encyclopaedia, losing some of its quirkiness in the process.[^quirky] [The IndieWeb is home to the playful and creative spirit of Generation X's internet.]({{< ref "/posts/2024-05-26-indieweb" >}}) As I become more responsible for keeping Wikipedia sober and encyclopaedic, maybe I will be drawn back to the indieweb community and this blog for genuine downtime.

[^quirky]: But not completely, as anyone who has wandered into Wikipedia's [userspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:User_pages) can attest.
