If you have a voice on the web, if you have any kind of 'following', no matter how large or small, what you say or do and, if called out, *what you don't say or do* will have an impact.

If you see the IndieWeb as merely a comfortable space for technical and creative hobbyists to build cool websites, if you don't want this space to become political, *that in itself is a political statement*. Only those who enjoy multiple layers of privilege – the Default Humans of this world – have the option of regarding 'politics' and 'identity' as discourse and not everyday lived reality.

We need people who are safe and privileged to help others find safety, to hold their peers to account, and to punch upwards. Because the rest of us are so exhausted just trying to get by.

Here are just a couple of examples from recent days of privileged members of the Indieweb community walking their talk.

- Adam Newbold: [Accountability](https://notes.neatnik.net/2025/01/accountability) and [Not Enough](https://notes.neatnik.net/2025/01/not-enough) – solidarity and discomfort over civility
- Ben Werdmuller: [In the face of this, who do you want to be?](https://werd.io/2025/in-the-face-of-this-who-do-you-want-to) – this goes beyond the IndieWeb; and [Seeking trans-friendly employers who sponsor visas](https://werd.io/2025/seeking-trans-friendly-employers-who-sponsor-visas) – a practical invitation to employers

To my fellow non-Americans, don't for a minute think that this is not our problem too. Sure, there have been fascists and despots leading many countries for most of human history. But seeing fascists win an  election in a supposedly democratic country will embolden their sympathisers in other democracies. Anarchists, decolonisers, and queer people have been teaching and supporting each other across international borders for decades. Fascists, racists, homophobes, and transphobes have been doing the same. The moderate middle must also decide where they stand, or show their position through their silence.
